How it's calculated

XIRR is the yearly rate at which every SIP instalment, grown to today, adds up to the current value. This calculator assumes equal instalments on the same date each month, the first one m months ago, and solves for the monthly rate r. XIRR = (1 + r)12 − 1. For irregular amounts or dates, use the XIRR function in a spreadsheet.

Why XIRR is the right return measure for a SIP

In a SIP, each instalment is invested at a different time, so a simple percentage gain on the total amount understates how hard your money has worked. The early instalments have been invested for years, while the latest ones only for weeks. XIRR accounts for this by finding the single yearly rate that turns every instalment into today's value.

For example, ₹10,000 a month for 36 months is ₹3.6 lakh invested. If it is worth ₹4.3 lakh today, the absolute return is about 19%, but the XIRR is about 11.9% a year. That annual figure is the one you can compare with an FD rate or a fund's CAGR.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between XIRR and CAGR?

CAGR measures growth of a single lump sum from start to end. XIRR handles many cash flows at different dates, such as SIP instalments, so it is the right measure for SIPs and irregular investing.

Why does my fund statement show a slightly different XIRR?

Statements use the exact date and amount of every transaction. This calculator assumes equal monthly instalments, so small differences in dates, skipped instalments or step-ups change the result.

Can XIRR be negative?

Yes. If your SIP is worth less than the total you put in, the XIRR is negative and shows the yearly rate at which value was lost.

How to use the XIRR Calculator

Enter monthly sip amount, months invested, current value. The calculator updates as you type and shows XIRR (annualised return), along with the figures behind it. It runs in your browser, so nothing you enter is sent anywhere. Use the Reset button to go back to the example values.
