How it's calculated

Money you can risk = capital × risk %. Shares = money you can risk ÷ (entry − stop-loss), limited by the shares your capital can buy. Risk : reward = (target − entry) ÷ (entry − stop-loss).

Sizing a trade by the risk you can afford

Position sizing decides how many shares to buy so that a losing trade costs you only a set part of your capital. You pick the percentage of capital you are willing to lose on one trade, then work back from your entry and stop-loss prices to the number of shares.

With the example values, risking 1% of ₹5,00,000 means ₹5,000 at risk. With an entry of ₹250 and a stop-loss of ₹240, each share risks ₹10, so you can buy 500 shares. At a target of ₹280, the reward is three times the risk.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate position size?

Divide the money you are willing to risk by the gap between your entry price and your stop-loss price. The calculator also caps it at what your capital can buy.

What risk per trade is sensible?

Many traders keep it to a small percentage of capital, such as 1% or 2%. The right figure depends on your own tolerance and strategy.

What is a risk : reward ratio?

It compares what you could gain to what you could lose. A ratio of 1 : 3 means the potential profit is three times the potential loss.
