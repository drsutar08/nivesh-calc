How it's calculated

Tax = taxable gain × rate. A holding kept longer than the long-term threshold is taxed at the long-term rate after the yearly exemption; otherwise the short-term rate applies. The defaults reflect rates for listed equity at the time of writing, so confirm the current rates before you rely on this.

Short-term and long-term capital gains on shares

Profit from selling shares is a capital gain. It is treated as short-term or long-term depending on how long you held the shares, and the two are taxed at different rates. Long-term gains on listed equity also have a yearly exemption limit.

Tax rules, rates and limits change, so treat the defaults here as editable starting values and confirm the current rules before you rely on a number. This calculator does not include surcharge, cess or transaction charges.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if a gain is short-term or long-term?

It depends on your holding period compared with the long-term threshold for that asset. Enter your holding period and the threshold to see which applies.

Can I set off a loss?

Capital losses can usually be set off against gains and carried forward under certain conditions. Check the current rules or ask a tax adviser.

Is this tax advice?

No. It is an estimate for planning. Speak to a qualified tax professional for your situation.
