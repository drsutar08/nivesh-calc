How it's calculated

Every month, interest is added to each loan (balance × yearly rate ÷ 12) and its minimum payment is made. With the avalanche or snowball method, the extra amount goes to one target loan, and when a loan is cleared its minimum payment rolls over to the next target. Avalanche targets the highest interest rate first, which saves the most interest. Snowball targets the smallest balance first, which clears loans sooner and can help motivation. The minimum-only case pays each loan its own minimum with no extra and no roll-over.

Avalanche or snowball: how to pay off several loans

When you have several loans, paying the minimum on all of them and putting every spare rupee towards one at a time clears the debt much faster. The question is which loan to target first.

The avalanche method targets the highest interest rate first, which saves the most money. The snowball method targets the smallest balance first, which gives quick wins that keep you going. For example, with a ₹2 lakh credit card at 36%, a ₹60,000 loan at 12% and a ₹5 lakh car loan at 9%, plus ₹5,000 extra a month, the avalanche method clears everything in about 2 years and 9 months and saves about ₹14,000 more than the snowball method.

Frequently asked questions

Which method is better?

Avalanche saves more interest. Snowball can be easier to stick with because you close accounts sooner. The best method is the one you will follow.

Should I pay off debt or invest?

High-interest debt, such as credit card balances, usually costs more than investments can safely earn, so clearing it first often makes sense. Keep an emergency fund too.

What if I cannot pay even the minimums?

Talk to your lenders early. Options include restructuring, a lower-rate loan to consolidate the debt, or converting card balances to EMIs.
