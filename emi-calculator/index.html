How it's calculated

EMI = P × i × (1 + i)n ÷ ((1 + i)n − 1), where i is the monthly interest rate and n is the tenure in months.

How your loan EMI is worked out

An Equated Monthly Instalment (EMI) is the fixed amount you pay every month to repay a loan. Each EMI covers the interest for the month and part of the principal. In the early years most of it is interest; later, most of it is principal.

With the example values, a ₹30 lakh loan at 8.5% for 20 years has an EMI of about ₹26,035 and costs roughly ₹32.5 lakh in interest, more than the amount you borrowed. A shorter tenure raises the EMI but cuts the total interest by a large amount.

Frequently asked questions

How is EMI calculated?

EMI = P × i × (1 + i)^n ÷ ((1 + i)^n − 1), where P is the loan, i the monthly interest rate and n the number of months.

How can I reduce the total interest?

Choose a shorter tenure, make part-prepayments, or find a lower rate. Try different tenures in the calculator to compare.

Does the EMI include processing fees?

No. Fees and insurance are separate and are not included.
