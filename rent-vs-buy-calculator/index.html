How it's calculated

Buyer: pays the EMI on (price − down payment) for the full tenure, then owns a home worth price × (1 + home value rise)years. Renter: invests the down payment plus buying costs on day one, pays rent that rises every year, and invests the gap whenever the EMI is higher than the rent. If rent becomes higher than the EMI, the buyer invests the gap instead. Both investments grow monthly at the return you enter, and net worth is compared at the end.

Rent or buy a home: comparing the numbers fairly

Buying a home builds equity, but it ties up a large down payment and adds buying costs and interest. Renting keeps that money free to invest. A fair comparison asks which choice leaves you with more wealth after the same number of years.

For example, for an ₹80 lakh home with 20% down, an 8.5% loan over 20 years and rent of ₹25,000 a month rising 5% a year, the renter who invests the down payment and the monthly gap at 10% ends about ₹1 crore ahead, if the home's value rises 5% a year. At ₹45,000 rent, buying ends well ahead instead.

Frequently asked questions

Why does renting often win on paper?

In many Indian cities, rent is a small fraction of the home's price, while the EMI is much larger. If the renter invests the difference steadily, it can grow to more than the home's value.

What does this calculator leave out?

Maintenance, property tax, home loan tax benefits, selling costs and the emotional value of owning a home. The renter also has to actually invest the difference every month.

Which inputs matter most?

The rent compared with the home price, the yearly rise in home value, and the return on investments. Try a few values for each to see when the answer changes.

How to use the Rent vs Buy Calculator

Enter home price, down payment, buying costs (stamp duty, registration), home loan rate (p.a.), years to compare (loan tenure), rent for a similar home, yearly rent increase, yearly rise in home value, return on investments (p.a.). The calculator updates as you type and shows Renting ends ahead by, along with the figures behind it. It runs in your browser, so nothing you enter is sent anywhere. Use the Reset button to go back to the example values.
