How it's calculated

Interest = amount funded × yearly rate × days held ÷ 365, where amount funded = position value × (100% − your margin). Net profit = (sell price − buy price) × quantity − interest. Return on your money = net profit ÷ your margin. Leverage multiplies both gains and losses. Brokerage, charges and any pledge fees are not included.

How the Margin Trading Facility works

With the Margin Trading Facility, your broker pays part of the cost of a delivery trade and charges interest on that amount for every day you hold the shares. You pay the margin, and the shares are pledged to the broker as security. Only stocks on the broker's approved list are eligible.

Leverage multiplies both sides. For example, buying ₹1 lakh of shares with ₹25,000 of your own money at 15% interest for 30 days costs about ₹925 in interest. A 10% rise gives a net profit of ₹9,075, which is about 36% on your money. But a 6% fall turns into a loss of about 28%.

Frequently asked questions

What happens if the share price falls?

If the value of your position falls below the required margin, the broker asks you to add money or shares (a margin call). If you do not, it can sell your shares to recover the loan.

How long can I hold an MTF position?

Usually as long as you keep paying interest and maintain the margin, but each broker sets its own rules. Interest keeps adding up every day you hold.

Is MTF suitable for long-term investing?

Rarely. The interest cost eats into returns over time, and leverage adds the risk of forced selling. It suits short holding periods when you understand the risk.
