How it's calculated

Lenders cap your total EMIs at a share of your income, called FOIR (fixed obligations to income ratio). EMI you can take on = income × FOIR − existing EMIs. Maximum loan = that EMI × (1 − (1 + i)−n) ÷ i, where i is the monthly rate and n is the number of months. Lenders also look at your credit score, age, job and the property's value, so the final offer can differ.

How lenders decide how much you can borrow

Lenders limit the share of your monthly income that can go towards EMIs. This is called the fixed obligations to income ratio (FOIR). Your existing EMIs come out of that limit first, and what is left decides the EMI, and therefore the loan, you can take on.

For example, with a net income of ₹1 lakh a month, ₹10,000 of existing EMIs and a 50% limit, you can take on a new EMI of ₹40,000. At 8.5% over 20 years, that supports a loan of about ₹46 lakh.

Frequently asked questions

How can I increase my loan eligibility?

Pay off existing loans, choose a longer tenure, add a co-applicant with income, or improve your credit score to get a better rate.

What FOIR do lenders use?

It varies by lender, income level and loan type. Many lenders use a limit somewhere around half of net income, and some allow more for higher incomes.

Is this the loan amount I will be offered?

Not necessarily. Lenders also check your credit history, age, job stability and, for home loans, the property's value, since a home loan is also capped at a share of the property's value.
