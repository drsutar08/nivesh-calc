How it's calculated

Sale price = price × (1 − discount ÷ 100). An extra discount applies to the already reduced price, so two discounts combine as 1 − (1 − d1)(1 − d2), which is always less than d1 + d2. For example, 20% off and then 10% off is 28% off in total, not 30%.

How to calculate a discount, and why discounts don't add up

To find a sale price, take the discount percentage off the original price. When a second discount is offered on top, such as an extra 10% at checkout, it applies to the already reduced price, not the original.

That is why two discounts never add up. For example, a ₹2,000 item at 20% off costs ₹1,600, and an extra 10% off brings it to ₹1,440. The total discount is 28%, not 30%.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a discount percentage?

Divide the amount saved by the original price and multiply by 100. For example, ₹500 off a ₹2,000 price is a 25% discount.

Is 'buy 1 get 1 free' a 50% discount?

Only if you wanted both items. It is 50% off the price per item, but you spend the full price of one.

Is the discount applied before or after GST?

Usually the discount is applied to the price first, and GST is charged on the reduced price. Check the bill to be sure.
