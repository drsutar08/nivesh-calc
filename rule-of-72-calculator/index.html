How it's calculated

Years to double ≈ 72 ÷ annual return %. The exact figure is ln 2 ÷ ln(1 + r). The shortcut is most accurate between 6% and 10%.

What the Rule of 72 tells you

The Rule of 72 is a quick way to estimate how long it takes an investment to double. Divide 72 by the annual return as a percentage and you get the approximate number of years. It works because of how compound growth behaves, and it needs no calculator.

At a 9% annual return, 72 ÷ 9 gives 8 years. The exact figure is about 8.04 years, so the shortcut is very close. It is most accurate for returns of roughly 6% to 10%. The calculator also shows the Rule of 114 for tripling and the Rule of 144 for growing four times.

Frequently asked questions

How do I use the Rule of 72?

Divide 72 by your expected yearly return. At 12%, 72 ÷ 12 gives about 6 years to double.

How accurate is the Rule of 72?

It is a close estimate for returns of about 6% to 10% and less exact outside that range. The calculator also shows the exact figure.

Can I use it for inflation?

Yes. At 6% inflation, prices double in about 12 years, so money loses half its buying power in that time.
