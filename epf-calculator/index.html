How it's calculated

Each month, your contribution and the employer's EPF share are added to the balance. Interest for the year is worked out on the monthly running balance at the yearly rate ÷ 12, and credited once at the end of the year. Your salary rises by the yearly increase you enter. The employer's pension (EPS) contribution is not part of the EPF balance and is not included. The EPFO declares the interest rate each year, and the default is the rate at the time of writing.

How your EPF balance grows

The Employees' Provident Fund is a retirement savings scheme for salaried employees. Each month you contribute a share of your basic pay and dearness allowance, and your employer contributes too. Part of the employer's share goes to the Employees' Pension Scheme (EPS), and the rest goes into your EPF account.

Interest is declared each year by the EPFO. It is calculated on the monthly running balance and credited at the end of the financial year. Because contributions rise with your salary and interest compounds every year, the balance grows much faster in later years.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the employer's EPF share lower than mine?

The employer's total contribution matches yours, but part of it goes to the pension scheme (EPS), which pays a monthly pension later instead of building your EPF balance.

Can I withdraw EPF before retirement?

Partial withdrawals are allowed for purposes such as a house, medical treatment, education or marriage, subject to conditions. You can also withdraw the balance after leaving a job, within the rules.

Should I contribute more through VPF?

The Voluntary Provident Fund lets you contribute more than the required share at the same interest rate. It suits people who want safe, long-term savings, but the money is locked in like EPF.
